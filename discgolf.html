    What Is Disc Golf?

    Disc golf is a flying disc sport played like traditional golf, but instead of clubs and balls, players throw a disc at a metal basket target. The goal is to complete each hole in the fewest throws possible. Courses wind through parks, forests, and open fields, making every round a mix of sport and nature walk.

    The sport has exploded in popularity over the last decade, with over 14,000 registered courses worldwide and millions of casual players who enjoy it as a free, low-barrier outdoor activity.

    The Three Main Disc Types

    Every player's bag contains a variety of discs. They fall into three main flight categories, each suited to different situations on the course:

    1. Distance Drivers
      • Designed for maximum distance off the tee
      • Sharper, narrower rim. This is harder to control for beginners
      • Popular models: Innova Destroyer, Discraft Zeus, Dynamic Discs Trespass
    2. Fairway / Control Drivers
      • More accurate than distance drivers, less distance potential
      • Great for wooded or technical holes
      • Popular models: Innova Leopard3, Latitude 64 River, Westside Burst
    3. Mid-Ranges
      • Versatile all-around disc for approach shots
      • Slower, more predictable flight. This is perfect for learning
      • Popular models: Discraft Buzzz, Innova Roc3, MVP Axis
    4. Putters
      • Used for short-range shots and finishing at the basket
      • Blunt nose, deep rim. Thid disc is very comfortable to grip
      • Popular models: Innova Aviar, Discraft Luna, Dynamic Discs Judge

    Famous Disc Golf Courses

    From scenic mountain layouts to challenging wooded tracks, these courses are on every serious player's bucket list:

    • Smugglers' Notch, Vermont — dense forest holes with dramatic elevation
      1. Book a tee time on busy summer weekends
      2. Bring an extra disc — woods can swallow them
    • Maple Hill, Massachusetts — host of major PDGA Pro Tour events
      1. Walk-on rounds available, but arrive early
      2. Elevation changes make club selection tricky
    • Idlewild, Kentucky — consistently ranked a top-10 course in the US
      1. Play it in the morning before heat and humidity peak
      2. No carts — wear comfortable hiking shoes
    • Fox Run Meadows, New York — wide-open parkland, beginner-friendly
